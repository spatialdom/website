# What is a land title in the Philippines, and what does it actually prove?

**3 min read · Last reviewed 2026-09-27**

A Philippine land title is the official certificate of registered ownership under the Torrens system. It is powerful evidence of the registered owner's rights over the described property, but reading a title is not the same as verifying every fact about the land on the ground.

## A title is the Registry of Deeds' record of registered land

Under the Property Registration Decree, an Original Certificate of Title is issued when land is first brought under the Torrens system. Later registered transfers lead to a Transfer Certificate of Title. The certificate identifies the registered owner, the land covered, and registrations that affect it.

That is why a photocopy handed to you by a seller should not be your final reference. The Land Registration Authority expressly lists due diligence for buying, selling and leasing property as a reason to obtain a Certified True Copy of title from the Registry of Deeds or through LRA eSerbisyo.

## What you can learn from the certificate

A current certified copy lets you check the title number, registered owner, property description and annotations appearing in the registration record. Mortgages, adverse claims, liens, restrictions and other registered interests may appear as annotations, so the back pages can be as important as the first page.

The Torrens system is designed so people can rely on the registration record, but that principle does not reward ignoring warning signs. Courts distinguish ordinary reliance on a clean face of title from situations where facts known to the buyer should trigger further inquiry.

## A title does not answer every property question

A certificate does not by itself tell you whether monuments on the ground are intact, whether occupants or access problems exist, whether a proposed use is allowed, or whether the parcel you were shown is the same parcel described in the title. Those questions require other records and, sometimes, field work.

The technical description can help you understand parcel geometry, but a browser plot is not a relocation survey. If the actual boundary matters to a purchase, construction or dispute, have a licensed Geodetic Engineer examine the records and site.

## Use the title as the center of due diligence, not the whole of it

Start with a fresh government-issued Certified True Copy, compare the owner's identity and title details with the transaction documents, read every annotation, and investigate inconsistencies before money changes hands. Then connect the title to tax records, survey information, possession, access and the legal documents for the sale as the transaction requires.

Parcel Plotter can help turn the title's technical description into a preliminary shape so you can understand what the text says. It does not authenticate the title or replace Registry of Deeds verification and professional survey work.
