# How do you verify a land title before buying property?

**2 min read · Last reviewed 2026-09-27**

> **Review copy.** Edit this Markdown file directly if you want to change the article. When you are satisfied, open [Issue #32](https://github.com/spatialdom/website/issues/32) and change the label from `insight:review` to `insight:approved`.

Start with a fresh Certified True Copy from the Land Registration Authority, compare it with the property and transaction documents, investigate annotations and inconsistencies, and verify the parcel on the ground when the transaction requires it.

## 1. Get the current government record

Ask for the Registry of Deeds, title type and title number, then obtain a Certified True Copy. LRA eSerbisyo accepts CTC requests for OCTs, TCTs and CCTs, and the LRA specifically lists property due diligence as a reason to request one.

Compare the CTC with the documents presented for the sale. Check the names, title number, property details, page count and annotations. Resolve discrepancies before treating the title as verified.

## 2. Read all annotations

Review mortgages, adverse claims, liens, restrictions, notices and other entries. Determine which are active, cancelled or require action. Registry of Deeds records exist so people dealing with registered land can inquire into registrations affecting it.

If an annotation is unfamiliar or a cancellation is being promised as part of the sale, have its legal effect and required steps checked before completing the transaction.

## 3. Match the title to the parcel

Compare the lot and survey identifiers, location, area and technical description with available survey records and the property being offered. A preliminary plot can reveal general shape and transcription questions, but it cannot establish monuments or boundaries on the ground.

Where fences, occupation, access, subdivision or boundary position matter, engage a licensed Geodetic Engineer for the appropriate professional review or survey.

## 4. Check what the title cannot show by itself

Inspect the property and consider possession, access, relevant tax records, land-use restrictions, project approvals and transaction requirements as applicable. A title is central evidence of registered ownership, but it is not a substitute for checking the rest of the transaction.

Warning signs change the level of inquiry required. Philippine court decisions distinguish normal reliance on a Torrens title from situations where known facts should make a buyer investigate further.

## 5. Verify before committing

A sensible sequence is: obtain the CTC; confirm the parties' authority to transact; understand annotations; match the title to the parcel and survey information; inspect possession and access; then have unresolved legal or boundary issues reviewed before final payment and registration.

Parcel Plotter can help with one narrow part of that process—understanding the technical description visually. It is not a title-authentication service or replacement for the Registry of Deeds, legal due diligence, or a professional survey.
